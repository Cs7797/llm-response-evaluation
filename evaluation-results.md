# LLM Response Evaluation and Factual Grounding

## 1. Overview

This evaluation compares responses generated by ChatGPT GPT-6 and Google Gemini against a fixed refund policy. The tests assess factual accuracy, source grounding, instruction-following, completeness, and clarity.

The evaluation focuses on how consistently each model follows the source material, handles ambiguous questions, and avoids making claims that are not supported by the policy.

## 2. Evaluation Setup

The reference document defines the following refund conditions:

- Refund requests must be made within 14 calendar days of delivery.
- Products must be unused and returned in their original packaging.
- Refund requests must include an order number.
- Approved refunds are processed within 5–7 business days after the returned product is received.
- The policy does not specify whether shipping charges are refundable.

Both models were evaluated using the same question sets and the same five-criterion scoring rubric. Each criterion was scored from 0 to 2, giving each response a maximum score of 10 per test.

## 3. Results Summary

| Test | ChatGPT GPT-6 | Google Gemini |
|---|---:|---:|
| Standard policy questions | 9/10 | 7/10 |
| False-premise detection | 10/10 | 10/10 |
| Unsupported claims and missing information | 10/10 | 10/10 |

These are manual scores for the specific responses tested. They should not be interpreted as a general ranking of either model.

## 4. Test 1: Standard Policy Questions

### Scores

| Criterion | ChatGPT GPT-6 | Google Gemini |
|---|---:|---:|
| Factual correctness | 2/2 | 1/2 |
| Source grounding | 2/2 | 1/2 |
| Instruction-following | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Completeness | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| Clarity | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| **Total** | **9/10** | **7/10** |

### Findings

#### 4.1 Refund request within 10 days

Both models correctly identified that a request made 10 days after delivery falls within the 14-calendar-day window. Both also referenced the unused-product and original-packaging requirements.

Neither response explicitly included the order-number requirement in this answer. This is a completeness gap because the policy lists the order number as a required part of a refund request, although the omission does not make the timing information incorrect.

#### 4.2 Refund request after 20 days

Both models correctly identified that a request made after 20 days falls outside the stated 14-day period. The responses applied the policy's timing requirement without introducing an alternative deadline.

#### 4.3 Required information

Both models correctly identified the order number as a required part of a refund request. The answers were direct and supported by the reference document.

#### 4.4 Refund processing time

Both models correctly stated that approved refunds are processed within 5–7 business days after the returned product is received. Neither response changed the stated processing period or its starting condition.

#### 4.5 Shipping-charge refundability

Both models correctly acknowledged that the policy does not specify whether shipping charges are refundable. Neither presented an unsupported shipping-refund rule as an established fact.

#### 4.6 Opened or used products

This question exposed a difference in how the models handled ambiguity.

ChatGPT distinguished between a product that has been used and one whose packaging has merely been opened. It noted that the policy requires products to be unused but does not explicitly state that opening the packaging alone makes a product ineligible.

Gemini answered that the product must be unused. Although this reflects the stated condition, it does not fully resolve the question's distinction between opening and using a product.

ChatGPT's answer was more precise because it separated the explicit policy requirement from information that remained unspecified.

#### 4.7 Claimed two-business-day guarantee

Both models correctly rejected the claim that the policy guarantees refunds within two business days. Both supplied the stated 5–7-business-day processing period and correctly connected it to receipt of the returned product.

#### 4.8 Missing order number

Both models correctly identified the order-number requirement.

ChatGPT also acknowledged that the policy does not describe exceptions or alternative verification methods. Gemini gave a categorical negative answer without identifying this uncertainty.

The policy supports the conclusion that a request without an order number does not meet the stated requirements. It does not establish whether an exception or alternative process is available. ChatGPT's response preserved this distinction more clearly.

### Test 1 Comparison

ChatGPT received 9/10, while Google Gemini received 7/10 under the evaluation rubric. The main difference was how the models handled questions where the source did not provide a complete answer. ChatGPT more consistently identified the limits of the available information, while Gemini's answers to the opened-product and missing-order-number questions were more categorical than the source fully justified.

## 5. Test 2: False-Premise Detection

### Objective

This test evaluates whether the models identify false assumptions in a question rather than accepting them as facts. The question incorrectly claims that all refund requests are guaranteed approval within two business days, including requests involving used products.

### Scores

| Criterion | ChatGPT GPT-6 | Google Gemini |
|---|---:|---:|
| Factual correctness | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Source grounding | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Instruction-following | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Completeness | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Clarity | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| **Total** | **10/10** | **10/10** |

### Findings

**ChatGPT GPT-6**

The response rejected the claim and identified three key issues: approval is not guaranteed, the stated processing period is incorrect, and used products do not meet the policy's conditions. It also identified information that the policy does not specify.

**Google Gemini**

The response also rejected the claim and correctly distinguished the processing time for approved refunds from a guarantee that every request will be approved. It identified the unused-product requirement and restated the relevant policy rules.

### Comparison

Both models detected the false premise and grounded their answers in the reference policy. ChatGPT explicitly discussed unspecified exceptions, while Gemini provided a concise explanation and summarized the relevant requirements.

Neither response accepted the invented two-business-day guarantee or suggested that used products qualify under the stated rules. Both received 10/10 under the rubric, and no meaningful difference was observed on this test.

## 6. Test 3: Unsupported Claims and Missing Information

### Objective

This test evaluates whether the models recognize when a reference document does not contain enough information to answer a question. It focuses on unsupported claims, missing details, and the tendency to invent policies or procedures.

### Test Questions

The models were asked about five details not established by the refund policy:

1. The percentage of refund requests approved.
2. Whether shipping charges are refunded automatically.
3. Whether extensions beyond the 14-day window are allowed.
4. Whether expedited refunds or additional fees are available.
5. Whether refunds are returned to the original payment method.

### Scores

| Criterion | ChatGPT GPT-6 | Google Gemini |
|---|---:|---:|
| Factual correctness | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Source grounding | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Instruction-following | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Completeness | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Clarity | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| **Total** | **10/10** | **10/10** |

### Findings

Both models correctly identified that the policy does not provide an approval rate, a rule for automatic shipping refunds, an extension process, an expedited refund option, or details about the payment method used for refunds.

Neither model invented specific percentages, fees, or procedures. Both distinguished between information explicitly stated in the policy and information that remained unknown.

The ChatGPT answers and Gemini answers reviewed for this test reached the same conclusions on the five questions.

### Comparative Assessment

Both models handled missing information well in this test. Their responses remained grounded in the reference policy and avoided unsupported assumptions. No meaningful difference in factual grounding was observed, and both received 10/10 under the rubric.

## 7. Key Takeaways

The three tests highlight several useful dimensions of LLM response quality:

- **Factual accuracy alone is insufficient.** Responses must preserve the conditions and qualifications stated in the source.
- **Unsupported claims require explicit attention.** A model should not fill gaps in a policy with assumptions.
- **Ambiguity handling matters.** Strong responses distinguish what the source establishes from what remains unknown.
- **Completeness should be assessed independently.** A response can be factually correct while omitting a relevant requirement.
- **False premises should be checked.** A model should verify assumptions in the question against the source.
- **Consistent scoring supports comparisons.** Applying the same rubric makes the reasoning behind each score easier to inspect.

## 8. Limitations and Future Work

This evaluation covers one fictional refund policy and three small test scenarios. Scores were assigned manually by a single reviewer, so inter-rater agreement was not measured. The results apply only to the responses tested and should not be interpreted as a general ranking of either model.

Further evaluation could include additional reference documents, more adversarial questions, coding tasks, and independent reviewers to assess scoring consistency across a wider range of scenarios.
