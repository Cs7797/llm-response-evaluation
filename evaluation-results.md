# LLM Response Evaluation and Factual Grounding

## 1. Overview

This evaluation compares responses generated by ChatGPT GPT-6 and Google Gemini against a fixed refund policy. Both models were given the same eight questions to assess factual accuracy, source grounding, instruction-following, completeness, and clarity.

The evaluation focuses on how consistently each model follows the source material, handles ambiguous questions, and avoids making claims that are not supported by the policy.

## 2. Evaluation Setup

The reference document defines the following refund conditions:

- Refund requests must be made within 14 calendar days of delivery.
- Products must be unused and returned in their original packaging.
- Refund requests must include an order number.
- Approved refunds are processed within 5–7 business days after the returned product is received.
- The policy does not specify whether shipping charges are refundable.

Both models were evaluated using the same question set and the same five-criterion scoring rubric. Each criterion was scored from 0 to 2, giving each response a maximum score of 10.

## 3. Evaluation Results

| Criterion | ChatGPT GPT-6 | Google Gemini |
|---|---:|---:|
| Factual correctness | 2/2 | 1/2 |
| Source grounding | 2/2 | 1/2 |
| Instruction-following | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| Completeness | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| Clarity | 2/2 | 2/2 |
| **Total** | **9/10** | **7/10** |

ChatGPT performed better under the selected rubric, primarily because it handled ambiguity more explicitly. Both models answered most questions accurately and followed the requested response format.

## 4. Detailed Findings

### 4.1 Refund Request Within 10 Days

Both models correctly identified that a request made 10 days after delivery falls within the 14-calendar-day window. Both also referenced the unused-product and original-packaging requirements.

Neither response explicitly included the order-number requirement in its answer. This represents a minor completeness gap rather than a factual error.

### 4.2 Refund Request After 20 Days

Both models correctly identified that a request made after 20 days falls outside the stated 14-day period. The responses applied the policy's timing requirement without introducing an alternative deadline.

### 4.3 Required Information

Both models correctly identified the order number as a required part of a refund request. The answers were direct and supported by the reference document.

### 4.4 Refund Processing Time

Both models correctly stated that approved refunds are processed within 5–7 business days after the returned product is received. Neither response changed the stated processing period or its starting condition.

### 4.5 Shipping-Charge Refundability

Both models correctly acknowledged that the policy does not specify whether shipping charges are refundable.

This is a positive grounding signal: neither model presented an unsupported shipping-refund rule as an established fact.

### 4.6 Opened or Used Products

This question exposed a difference in how the models handled ambiguity.

ChatGPT distinguished between a product that has been used and one whose packaging has merely been opened. It correctly noted that the policy requires products to be unused but does not explicitly state that opening the packaging alone makes a product ineligible.

Gemini answered that the product must be unused. Although this reflects the stated condition, it does not fully resolve the question's distinction between opening and using a product.

ChatGPT's answer was more precise because it separated the explicit policy requirement from information that remained unspecified.

### 4.7 Claimed Two-Business-Day Guarantee

Both models correctly rejected the claim that the policy guarantees refunds within two business days. Both supplied the stated 5–7-business-day processing period and correctly connected it to receipt of the returned product.

### 4.8 Missing Order Number

Both models correctly identified the order-number requirement.

However, ChatGPT also acknowledged that the policy does not describe exceptions or alternative verification methods. Gemini gave a categorical negative answer without identifying this uncertainty.

The policy supports the conclusion that a request without an order number does not meet the stated requirements. It does not establish whether an exception or alternative process is available. ChatGPT's response preserved this distinction more clearly.

## 5. Comparative Assessment

ChatGPT received 9/10, while Google Gemini received 7/10 under the evaluation rubric.

The main difference was not basic factual recall, since both models answered most questions correctly. It was the handling of questions where the source did not provide a complete answer.

ChatGPT more consistently identified the limits of the available information. Gemini's responses were clear and generally accurate, but two answers were more categorical than the source fully justified.

The preferred response for this test is **ChatGPT GPT-6**, based on its stronger handling of ambiguity and source limitations.

## 6. Key Takeaways

The evaluation highlights several useful dimensions of LLM response quality:

- **Factual accuracy alone is insufficient.** Responses must also preserve the conditions and qualifications stated in the source.
- **Unsupported claims require explicit attention.** A model should not fill gaps in a policy with assumptions.
- **Ambiguity handling matters.** Strong responses distinguish what the source establishes from what remains unknown.
- **Completeness should be assessed independently.** A response may be factually correct while omitting a relevant requirement.
- **Consistent scoring improves comparisons.** Applying the same rubric to both models makes the reasoning behind the preference easier to inspect.

## 7. Limitations and Future Work

This evaluation covers one fictional refund policy and eight questions. Scores were assigned manually by a single reviewer, so inter-rater agreement was not measured. The results apply only to the responses tested and should not be interpreted as a general ranking of either model.

Further evaluation could include additional reference documents, more adversarial questions, coding tasks, and independent reviewers to assess scoring consistency across a wider range of scenarios.
